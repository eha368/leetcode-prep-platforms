# LeetCode alternatives: pick a prep platform that matches your timeline, budget, and learning style

Most people searching for LeetCode alternatives aren't angry at LeetCode. They're stuck. You open the problem list, see 3,000+ questions, pick one at random, fail it, read a solution you don't quite follow, and repeat. Two weeks later you can't tell whether you've improved.

LeetCode is good at what it is: a huge problem bank with company tags, contests, and discussion threads. What it doesn't give you is a defined endpoint or an explanation of *why* a given approach applies. That's the gap most alternatives are built around, and it's why the "right" alternative depends entirely on which part of interview prep is breaking for you.

So this isn't a ranked list. It's a way to match the tool to the bottleneck, plus current pricing for one of the pattern-first options, AlgoMonster, so you can do the math yourself.

## First, name the bottleneck

Four different problems get filed under "LeetCode alternatives":

**You don't know what to study next.** Typical of self-taught developers and people prepping for their first internship. You have no roadmap, so you default to whatever problem shows up in a YouTube thumbnail.

**You can solve problems you've seen, but freeze on new ones.** This is pattern recognition — the skill interviews actually test. You look at a problem and can't tell whether it's a sliding window, a heap, or two pointers until you've burned 15 minutes.

**You understand the patterns, but you're slow.** You need volume and reps under time pressure. LeetCode's free tier is genuinely good here.

**Your loop has rounds LeetCode doesn't cover.** System design, behavioral, SQL, data analysis. No amount of array grinding fixes a system design round.

Fixing the wrong bottleneck is how people end up paying for two subscriptions and finishing neither.

## If your bottleneck is pattern recognition

AlgoMonster is the alternative most explicitly built around "learn the pattern, not the problem." The curriculum is organized around roughly 48 core patterns — two pointers, sliding window, binary search variants, BFS/DFS, dynamic programming, and so on — and every lesson follows the same arc: explain the reasoning, give you a generalized template, then make you apply it to a few variations with different twists.

Two features do more work than they sound like they should.

**Decision flowcharts.** These walk you through questions like *is the input sorted?*, *am I looking for a contiguous subarray?*, *is this a tree or a graph?* to narrow down which pattern applies. It's the thing you're supposed to be doing silently in your head during an interview, written down.

**Speedrun mode.** Whatever you've learned gets revisited on a clock, which is closer to interview conditions than solving at your own pace.

The site currently lists 500+ lessons across 6 courses, 1,000+ illustrations, and 3,000+ editorials, with solutions in Python, Java, JavaScript, C++, Go, and Haskell. Company question banks pull from real questions at major tech companies and get updated monthly. AI features are embedded rather than bolted on: you can select any passage and get a plain-language explanation, or submit code and have it point at the error and explain the fix, with recurring mistake patterns surfacing over time. Foundation and LLM-building courses are bundled in, and the yearly plan includes a member discount on live bootcamps. Specialized partner courses like Quant Interview Prep are sold separately.

If you want to see whether the flowchart-and-template style clicks before paying, the free tier is the honest test.

## Annual or lifetime: do the arithmetic

Two numbers, then decide.

- Yearly: $99, renews.
- Lifetime: $189, once.

If you prep once and then stop thinking about algorithms for three years, yearly is fine — you pay $99 and you're done. But the lifetime plan passes the yearly plan at about the two-year mark ($99 × 2 = $198, versus $189 once). Engineers who change jobs every two or three years end up prepping repeatedly, and that's the profile the lifetime plan is priced for. Existing users also describe staying on the platform to revisit patterns before specific rounds, which stretches the value past a single job search.

The counterargument is real: if you finish the curriculum in six weeks and never open it again, you've paid $90 extra over a single year of access. That's a judgment about your own job-hopping cadence, not a comparison of features.

## What AlgoMonster won't do for you

Being clear about this saves money.

**No video.** Everything is text and interactive diagrams. That's a deliberate choice, and the pitch is that reading and coding beat passive watching. It also means anyone who learns from walkthroughs on screen — the NeetCode and AlgoExpert style — will find this format dry. If you know you're a video learner, don't buy it hoping to adapt.

**Problem volume is small.** The curriculum is in the low hundreds of problems, not thousands. Pattern recognition is necessary but not sufficient; the fluency that comes from solving hundreds of problems is a separate thing, and most candidates supplement with LeetCode's free tier for reps.

**System design is introductory.** Load balancing, caching, sharding-level material. L5+ loops at large companies expect considerably more depth, which means a second resource.

**Behavioral prep is thin.** Enough to know the format, not enough to master STAR-format answers against specific company criteria.

**No live interview simulation.** Nobody is watching, no follow-up questions, no timer unless you use Speedrun. Pattern knowledge is what you build here; pressure handling is something you build elsewhere, with mock interviews.

## Free alternatives worth keeping in the stack

None of these replace a paid course, but a few are close to mandatory.

- **LeetCode's free tier.** The problem bank and company tags without Premium. This is where pattern knowledge turns into speed.
- **NeetCode's free walkthroughs.** A curated list with explanations, useful when one specific pattern won't stick and you want it drawn out on screen.
- **Codeforces.** Rated contests, free, punishing in a useful way if your weakness is speed rather than recognition.
- **Exercism.** Free mentored exercises in 80+ languages. Human feedback on style and edge cases, which no automated judge gives you.
- **GeeksforGeeks.** Topic-by-topic written explanations when you need a single concept re-explained differently.
- **Pramp-style peer mock interviews.** The cheapest way to simulate someone watching you think out loud.

## Two study stacks that actually work

**Six weeks, interview already scheduled.** AlgoMonster's patterns at 1–2 hours a day, roughly 4–6 weeks to cover the curriculum, then LeetCode's free tier for the two weeks before the loop to build speed on the patterns you just learned. Add one mock interview a week from week three. The point of the paid course here is that it has an endpoint — you can't finish 3,000 problems in six weeks, and pretending otherwise is how people arrive unprepared.

**Three months, senior-level loop.** Use the pattern course for the first month to rebuild the DSA foundation, then split the remaining time between volume practice on the free bank and a dedicated system design resource, since the coding round is not the only one you'll face at that level.

## FAQ

**Is AlgoMonster a replacement for LeetCode?**
For learning, mostly yes. For practice volume, no. The common pattern is patterns first, then LeetCode for reps.

**Does AlgoMonster have a monthly plan?**
Not on the current plans page. It's annual or lifetime, with a free tier to preview the content.

**Do I need to know how to code already?**
Yes. It assumes you're fluent in at least one language and teaches selection — which structure and approach to reach for. If you're still learning what a hash map is, the included Foundation course helps, but start with fundamentals elsewhere.

**Is the lifetime plan a subscription trap?**
No. It's a single payment covering current content and future updates, with no renewal.

**Should I buy it if I'm targeting senior roles at big tech?**
Only alongside system design and behavioral prep. Buying it alone and skipping those rounds is the most predictable way to under-prepare.

**What if I'm not sure about the format?**
Use the free tier for a week. The text-and-diagram style either works for you within an hour or two, or it doesn't, and you'll know before spending anything.
